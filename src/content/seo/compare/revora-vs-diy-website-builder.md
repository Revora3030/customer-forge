# Revora vs DIY website builders

Drag-and-drop builders are very good at one thing: producing a clean website for little money. A page is not the same as a pipeline, though. After the visitor arrives, someone still has to reply, quote, schedule, follow up and ask for the review. This page compares the two fairly, so you can decide which job you need done.

Builder prices come from each company's own site, checked October 31, 2026, on annual billing unless noted. Confirm before buying.

## Side by side

| | DIY builder | Revora |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Wix about $17 to $159/month (Light to Business Elite) on yearly billing. Squarespace $19 to $99/month on annual billing. Wix yearly plans are paid upfront. Check GoDaddy's current plan prices | $350 setup, first month free, then $100/month. Cancel anytime |

| Online booking | Included on some tiers: Wix Core and above. Squarespace uses Acuity Scheduling, a separate subscription. Check GoDaddy's current plans | Built in, tied to your services and durations |

| CRM and follow-up | Not listed on the plans pages we reviewed | Every form, quote and booking from your site in one pipeline, with scheduled follow-up |
| Review requests | You send them yourself | A ready-made automation sends them after you mark a job complete |
| Your time | Writing, building, maintaining | Setup and a few decisions; the system handles routine work |
| Free trial | Squarespace has one with no card; GoDaddy and Wix have free plans | 3 days of full access with no card |

## When a DIY builder is the better choice

- You get most of your work by referral and need a credible page to point people to.
- You enjoy building and have the time.
- You want the lowest possible monthly cost. A builder can cost under a third of Revora's monthly fee (for example Wix Light or Core, or Squarespace Basic or Core).
- You want an online store, which builders are built for.
- You already have a site you like and handle leads well yourself.

If that is you, a builder is cheaper and perfectly adequate. Squarespace and Wix are both good at what they do.

## When Revora is the better choice

- You lose leads between "form submitted" and "customer booked."
- You are on a job all day and cannot answer quickly.
- You want instant quotes and booking on the same site.
- You do not want to write and maintain a dozen service and area pages.
- You want every lead in one list with a next step.

## The real cost of DIY

The sticker price is not the whole cost. Count the weekends spent building, the add-ons you may need for quoting and follow-up and the leads that wait a day for a reply. Then count Revora's first year honestly: $350 plus 11 months at $100 is $1,450. If your average job is worth several hundred dollars, a handful of recovered leads changes the math. If it is worth less, or you rarely lose leads, a builder is the better value.

## Common questions

**I already have a website. Is it wasted?** No. Your content, photos and domain can move over. What changes is that the site is attached to quoting, booking, CRM and follow-up.

**Can I still edit my own site?** Yes. The visual builder lets you edit sections, text, images, colors and pages, and changes can be rolled back.

**Will this rank better than a builder site?** No one can promise a specific Google position. Both depend on useful pages, accurate information and real reviews.

**Do I own the site?** Yes. The site, customer list and data are yours.

**Try it before you pay.** Revora gives you 3 days of full access with no card. Setup is $350, your first month is free, then it is $100 a month, and you can cancel anytime. [See pricing](/pricing) or [start your system](/get-started).
