# Revora vs buying leads

Lead marketplaces and Revora solve different problems. A marketplace can fill your schedule this week. A system of your own builds a channel that keeps working when you stop paying for leads. Many owners use both at different times.

We have not named specific marketplaces here, because each has its own terms. The points below are common patterns. Check the terms of any marketplace you use before relying on them.

## Side by side

| | Lead marketplace | Revora |
|---|---|---|
| Who the lead belongs to | Depends on the marketplace. Some sell the same request to several businesses | You. The inquiry arrives through your own site and stays in your CRM |
| How you pay | Often per lead or per contact. Check how refunds and bad leads are handled | $350 setup, first month free, then $100/month, regardless of how many inquiries arrive |
| What you own afterward | Typically a record of what you paid | A site, a customer list, review history and analytics by page, city and channel |
| Speed to start | Can start quickly | Setup takes longer, and 3 days of full access come first |
| Response pressure | If the lead is shared, you may be racing other businesses | A ready-made automation can send a first reply, then scheduled follow-up |
| Repeat business | Depends on whether the customer record is yours | Past customers sit in your CRM with reminders and review requests |

## When buying leads is the better choice

When your schedule is empty this week, buying a few leads is a legitimate short-term bridge. A new business with no site, no reviews and no reputation sometimes needs that bridge. The risk is leaving it as the only channel: costs can rise as competition grows, and you rent the customer relationship instead of owning it.

## When your own channel is the better choice

- You want inquiries that do not cost more per lead as you grow.
- You want customers who found you by name, with a relationship that is yours.
- You want reviews and a site that keep working when you pause spending.
- You want to see which pages and cities bring booked jobs.

## How to run both

You do not have to stop buying leads. Keep any source that is clearly paying for itself, route those leads into one pipeline so nothing is lost and track the cost per booked job for each source. As your own channel grows, move spend to where the numbers are best.

A simple scorecard for each channel:

1. Total spend this month.
2. Booked jobs from that channel.
3. Average job value.
4. Spend divided by booked jobs.

No channel, including ours, can promise a specific number of leads.

## Common questions

**Should I stop buying leads immediately?** No. Keep whatever is currently paying for itself, point the leads into your pipeline and let your own channel take over as it grows.

**Does Revora generate leads for me?** Revora builds your website, quotes, booking, follow-up and local SEO foundation so that people who search for you can reach and book you. It does not buy or resell leads, and no one can promise results.

**How do I know if a marketplace is worth it?** Count booked jobs and the cost per booked job, not leads received.
