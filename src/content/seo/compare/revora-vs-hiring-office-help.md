# Revora vs hiring office help

Most owners hit the same wall: the phone, the quotes, the scheduling and the follow-up outgrow the truck. The usual answer is to hire someone. The other answer is to make part of that work stop existing. They are not exclusive, and for many businesses the best answer is a bit of each.

## Side by side

| | Part-time office help | Revora |
|---|---|---|
| Hours covered | Their working hours | A first-reply automation can respond when a form, quote or booking request arrives, and the request is logged in your pipeline |
| Cost | Wages, payroll costs, training and time to hire. Amounts vary by area and role, so price it locally | $100/month after a $350 setup and a free first month |
| Judgment | Strong. A person can handle odd requests and difficult customers | Limited. The system handles routine steps and passes the rest to you |

| Volume | Needs more hours or more people as you grow | The same process whether 10 or 200 inquiries arrive |

## When office help is the better choice

When the work genuinely needs judgment: complex bids, difficult customers, site visits, purchasing, vendor calls and anything that needs a human voice. If most of your inquiries arrive by phone and customers expect a person, a person will serve them better than a form.

## When a system is the better choice

When the work is repetitive: replying first, sending a price range, chasing unanswered quotes, requesting reviews, keeping the list up to date. A system does those steps the same way every time, and your hours (or a hire's) go to work that needs a person.

## Use both

A common setup: the system handles the first reply, price ranges, booking, follow-up and review requests. A part-time person handles calls, tricky quotes and scheduling changes. The system's CRM gives that person a clean list instead of a pile of messages.

## A quick test

Write down every task you or your office help did last week. Mark each one **routine** (same steps every time) or **judgment** (a person has to decide). If most of the list is routine, try a system first. If most of it is judgment, hire.

## Common questions

**Will this replace my office manager?** Not the judgment part. It removes the repetitive part, so a person's hours go to work that needs a person.

**Does it answer the phone?** No. It handles online inquiries, quotes, booking and follow-up. If you need calls answered, that is a human or a separate service.

**What if I only have a few leads a month?** Then a system may be more than you need. Do the math on missed leads before you pay for anything.
